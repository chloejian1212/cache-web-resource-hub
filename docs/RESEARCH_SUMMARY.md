# Research Summary

[Project overview](../README.md) · [Case study](CASE_STUDY.md)

## Research purpose

Understand how visitors with different backgrounds discover, evaluate, and use climate, health, and equity resources, and identify changes that could make the CACHE website easier to navigate.

Research findings below are paraphrased at theme level. Raw transcripts, recordings, participant contact details, and identifiable quotations are not included.

## Main research questions

1. What does a visitor expect CACHE to provide?
2. How does a visitor decide that a resource is credible and relevant?
3. When does the visitor need a quick overview, and when do they need depth?
4. Which formats fit the visitor’s work or learning context?
5. Does a topic, audience, or format label help the visitor choose a path?
6. What makes a resource page feel overwhelming or inefficient?

## Qualitative findings

### 1. Quick entry and deep exploration are both legitimate needs

Some response notes favor concise overviews and a small curated selection. Others favor a larger collection, especially for research, with a way to narrow the choices. A layered structure addresses this tension more directly than assuming that every visitor wants the same amount of material.

**Implication:** Show a limited, clearly identified introduction, then provide additional resources organized for scanning.

### 2. Context supports trust and selection

The response notes identify sources, dates, short descriptions, and recognizable affiliations as useful signals. Visitors also want to understand the organization’s purpose and the people behind it.

**Implication:** Put the source and purpose of a resource near its link. Make organizational context accessible without forcing it into every resource description.

### 3. Format preferences depend on the task

Practitioner personas emphasize patient-ready summaries and printable materials. Novice response notes show varied preferences for articles, videos, and fact sheets, alongside frustration with paywalls, forced downloads, and dense text.

**Implication:** Provide a brief on-page description and label formats explicitly. Keep printable material available without making it the only way to understand the content.

### 4. Audience groupings need to preserve discoverability

Audience-based entry points appear in the persona recommendations. Some novice responses support these groupings, while others worry that they could restrict what they see. Topic-based browsing also appears as a preference.

**Implication:** Treat audience labels as guidance rather than an exclusive partition. Retain a common route through the topic collection. The published site uses a topic-based entry model. Whether additional audience guidance improves discovery remains a question for follow-up testing.

### 5. Local and actionable information matters in use

Professional persona materials emphasize local relevance and resources that can support immediate patient conversations. Other notes connect usefulness to education, research, advocacy, and community action.

**Implication:** Include geographic context where it is actually supported by a resource, and state what the visitor can learn or do with it. Do not label a national resource as North Carolina-specific without justification.

## Connection to the published website

The [live website review](LIVE_SITE.md) adds delivery evidence to the research record:

- The hub provides topic entry points rather than requiring an audience identity.
- Topic pages place selected introductory material before further-resource groups.
- Several pages group additional material by format.
- The Education & Training web-resource list was expanded successfully.

These observations are consistent with several research directions, but they do not prove causation or establish that all research recommendations were implemented. No custom audience or regional filter is claimed.

## Evidence limits

- These are qualitative themes, not statistically representative estimates.
- A planned recruitment target is not treated as the completed participant count.
- Response numbering is not used to reconstruct a total sample across files.
- The existence of a questionnaire or testing script does not prove that every proposed measure was collected or scored.
- No final SUS score or before-and-after comparison is reported.
- Numeric charts embedded in the analytics document were not extracted or used to claim an impact.
- The April feedback supplies iteration context; the current public website separately confirms visible delivery. Exact implementation dates and version history are not established.
- A live interface confirms publication, not an improvement in user performance or satisfaction.

## Proposed follow-up evaluation

The following is a new evaluation outline for future work, not a report of a completed study.

| Task | Observable outcome | Useful observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explain the website’s purpose and intended audience. | Participant forms a reasonable interpretation without prompting. | Which content or label establishes the purpose? |
| Find a resource for a realistic work or study situation. | Participant reaches a relevant destination and explains the choice. | Where do they hesitate, backtrack, or abandon a path? |
| Open a topic’s further-resource group and choose a useful item. | Participant finds the control and identifies a relevant destination. | Is the introductory/further-reading distinction understandable? |
| Move from a topic page to the hub or a related topic. | Participant reaches the intended destination. | Does the link label match the page reached? |
| Find how to receive updates or become involved. | Participant can distinguish the available options. | Does the action label match the destination? |

Record task success, time on task, navigation path, assistance, and a brief explanation of the participant’s choice. If comparing versions, keep tasks, starting conditions, device context, and success definitions consistent, and report the actual sample and limitations.

The persona materials suggest finding useful information quickly as a success criterion. A time target should be defined and tested; it must not be reported as an achieved result until measured.
