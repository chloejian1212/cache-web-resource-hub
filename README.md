# CACHE Web Resource Hub

**From a growing resource collection to a published, topic-based learning experience.**

[View live website](https://carolinaadvocates.org/resources) · [Education & Training page](https://carolinaadvocates.org/resources-et) · [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md)

A collaborative UX research, information architecture, content design, and Squarespace website project for **Carolina Advocates for Climate, Health & Equity (CACHE)**, developed through the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill’s INLS 779 practicum.

The delivered resource hub gives visitors topic-based entry points, introductory learning content, and expandable lists for further exploration. This repository presents the research and design case study, with screenshots and links to the published website.

## The challenge

CACHE serves visitors with different information needs: practitioners looking for useful material, students beginning a topic, and people seeking more detailed resources. The design challenge was to make the collection approachable without removing the depth that more experienced visitors needed.

Stakeholder discussions and user research informed the work on navigation, resource organization, credibility, and practical content use.

**Design question:** How might we help visitors identify a useful starting point, then explore more deeply when they need to?

These published destinations differ from the early six-category planning taxonomy; the [information architecture document](docs/INFORMATION_ARCHITECTURE.md) explains the distinction.

The topic-page pattern combines:

- A short introduction that establishes the scope of the topic.
- A prominent introductory learning section, with selected resources and explanatory content.
- Expandable lists for additional resources. Several topics group these by articles, websites, and videos; Education & Training uses a web-resource list.
- Links to related topics and a route back toward the resource hub.

The Education & Training resource list was opened successfully during the live review. A few navigation defects remain and are recorded in the [live-site notes](docs/LIVE_SITE.md).

## Research → design → delivery

| Research or stakeholder input | Design direction | Visible in the published experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visitors need different levels of depth. | Separate a clear starting point from further reading. | Introductory learning content precedes expandable resource lists. |
| Dense collections can make the next step unclear. | Make the collection easier to enter by topic. | The hub offers topic destinations with images and short descriptions. |
| Resource formats suit different tasks. | Make additional material easier to scan by type. | Several topic pages separate articles, web resources, and videos. |
| The organization needs to maintain the site after the practicum. | Use the existing platform and document ongoing care. | The site is published on the client’s platform; this repository includes a recommended maintenance workflow. |

This connects research themes to observed design choices. It does not imply that a measured improvement has been established for each choice.

## Outcome and reflection

The project has a public resource experience that readers can explore directly. Its clearest observable outcome is a structured route from a topic overview to introductory material and further resources.

The work taught me to treat resource design as a series of decisions: help people choose a topic, recognize a useful starting point, and continue without losing their place. Content structure and ongoing maintenance matter as much as the initial page layout.

No final usability score, task-time improvement, conversion uplift, or traffic increase is claimed without a verified comparison. The live review confirms visible delivery, not a complete accessibility audit or the validity of every external resource.
